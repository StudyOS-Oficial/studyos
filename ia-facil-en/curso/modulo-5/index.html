MODULE 05 · 10 LESSONS

Get better answers

Move beyond basic prompts. Learn how to control depth, tone, structure and reasoning, break complex tasks into stages and revise results deliberately.

LESSON 1

Be specific about the result

A strong request describes the outcome you want, not just the topic. Topic: 'healthy eating'. Result: 'Create a seven-day dinner plan with five ingredients per meal and a shopping list.'

Practical idea: Ask yourself: 'What would a useful answer look like on my screen?' Then describe that result to the AI.
LESSON 2

Break complex tasks into steps

Large tasks often become easier when divided into stages. Ask for an outline before the full answer, or have AI analyse the problem before proposing a solution.

Practical idea: For a presentation: first request the structure, then the key message of each slide, then the speaker notes. You can approve or change each stage.
LESSON 3

Control the level of detail

Tell AI whether you need a quick answer, a beginner explanation or a deep analysis. 'Explain simply' and 'analyse in depth' are very different instructions.

Practical idea: You can also set a practical limit: 'Give me the answer in under 300 words, then add a section called If you want to go deeper.'
LESSON 4

Control tone and voice

Tone changes how information feels. Specify whether you want formal, friendly, warm, neutral, persuasive, concise or conversational language.

Practical idea: For personal communication, preserve your voice: 'Keep my wording natural and do not make me sound like a company.' For professional writing, specify the audience and level of formality.
LESSON 5

Ask for the right structure

Structure is a powerful form of control. Request headings, bullets, tables, checklists, timelines or a decision matrix according to the job.

Practical idea: A useful prompt is: 'Start with a three-line summary. Then give me the detailed steps. Finish with the three most important risks.'
LESSON 6

Use examples and counterexamples

Examples show what you mean. Counterexamples show what you do not want. Together they reduce ambiguity.

Practical idea: For instance: 'Give me titles that sound informative, not sensational. Good: How to Plan a Weekend Trip. Avoid: The ULTIMATE Secret That Will Change Your Life.'
LESSON 7

Request a review before the final version

For important work, ask AI to inspect a draft for gaps before rewriting it. You can ask it to check clarity, completeness, contradictions and unsupported claims.

Practical idea: Prompt: 'First identify weaknesses in this draft. Do not rewrite it yet. Group the issues into clarity, structure and factual claims.' Then decide which changes to make.
LESSON 8

Handle uncertainty honestly

When the answer depends on missing information, tell AI not to guess. Ask it to identify uncertainty and list what needs checking.

Practical idea: Try: 'If you are not sure, say so. Separate facts, reasonable assumptions and suggestions.' This makes the output easier to evaluate.
LESSON 9

Create reusable prompt templates

If you repeatedly do the same kind of task, create a template with placeholders. This saves time and makes your results more consistent.

Practical idea: Template: 'I need help with [TASK]. Audience: [AUDIENCE]. Context: [CONTEXT]. Constraints: [LIMITS]. Desired format: [FORMAT]. Ask me up to three questions if something essential is missing.'
LESSON 10

Final practical challenge

Choose a task that normally produces mediocre AI answers. Write a structured prompt with task, context, constraints, format and tone. Generate a result, review it, then request a revised version based on your critique.

Practical idea: Save the final prompt as your own template. The real skill is not memorising a formula—it is learning to communicate what you need clearly and improve the result deliberately.
Do it now: Apply the technique to one real situation from your own life. Keep the task simple enough to finish in 10–15 minutes.
FINAL CHECK

Put the module into practice

Before moving on, answer these questions for yourself:

  1. What is the most useful idea from this module?
  2. Where could you apply it this week?
  3. What would you need to verify before trusting the result?

Remember: AI is most useful when you stay in control, provide good instructions and review what comes back.
