MODULE 10 · 10 LESSONS

Create and communicate with AI

Write, summarise, explain, generate ideas and improve your communication while keeping your own voice and judgement.

LESSON 1

Start from your own idea

AI is most useful when it helps develop something you already care about. Give it your rough notes, objective or message rather than asking it to invent your entire voice.

In depth: AI is usually more useful when it develops material you care about rather than inventing your entire viewpoint. Give it rough notes, an objective or an initial idea and ask it to structure or improve them. This keeps your ownership of the message while using AI for speed and editing. Giving AI a point of view, audience and purpose helps it support your intent instead of filling gaps with generic language. Keep the original alongside the revision so you can compare what changed.
Try it: Paste a messy paragraph and ask: 'Keep my meaning and voice, but make this clearer.'
LESSON 2

Draft emails and messages

Tell AI who you are writing to, why you are writing and the tone you want. Then read every sentence before sending it.

In depth: A good draft depends on recipient, purpose and tone. Tell AI what you need to communicate and what relationship you have with the reader. Ask for a concise draft, then check every sentence before sending it because only you know the full context. Before using a draft, check names, facts, promises and tone against the relationship. A polished message can still imply a commitment or disclose context you did not intend to share.
Try it: Try: 'Write a short, friendly message declining the invitation while making it clear I appreciate being asked.'
LESSON 3

Rewrite without losing your voice

Ask for specific changes: shorter, clearer, warmer, more formal or less repetitive. Avoid generic instructions such as 'make it better' when your goal is specific.

In depth: Rewriting can improve clarity without replacing personality. Specify what should change—length, grammar, structure or tone—and what should remain. Then review the result for phrases that no longer sound like you. Compare the revision with your original for meaning, emphasis and personality. If the text feels generic, ask for a narrower edit such as grammar only or shorter sentences while preserving your wording.
Try it: Keep a sentence or phrase that sounds like you. This gives the AI a style anchor.
LESSON 4

Summarise long information

AI can turn a long text into a summary, action list or set of key questions. If accuracy matters, compare the summary with the original.

In depth: A summary is a transformation, not a perfect copy. Qualifications and exceptions can disappear when text is compressed. Ask for layers such as an overview, key actions and unresolved questions, then compare important summaries with the original. Ask what the summary left out, including caveats, disagreement and conditions. For a decision or important instruction, return to the original passage rather than relying on a compressed paraphrase.
Try it: Prompt: 'Summarise this in five points. Then list any deadlines, actions and uncertainties separately.'
LESSON 5

Brainstorm ideas

AI is excellent for generating possibilities quickly. Ask for quantity first, then filter by your criteria.

In depth: Separate generation from evaluation. First ask for many possibilities, then introduce criteria such as cost, audience, originality or feasibility. This prevents the first plausible idea from becoming the default simply because it appeared first. Generate a varied set before evaluating it, then use the same criteria for every option. The first suggestion is not automatically the best; compare feasibility and fit with your actual goal.
Try it: Try: 'Give me 20 ideas for a family activity on a rainy day. Then group them by cost and age suitability.'
LESSON 6

Create a presentation or document

Start with structure before prose. Ask for a clear outline, review it, then expand the sections you actually need.

In depth: Strong documents benefit from structure before prose. Define purpose, audience and key message, create an outline and review it before asking AI to expand the sections. This reduces filler and keeps the document focused. An outline lets you catch missing sections and weak logic before investing in polished prose. Check that each section contributes to the purpose, and remove material that only makes the document longer.
Try it: This reduces generic filler and gives you control over the final message.
LESSON 7

Translate and adapt language

AI can help translate or adapt a message for a different audience. For important legal, medical or contractual text, use qualified translation or professional review when precision is critical.

In depth: Translation involves meaning, register and cultural context, not only word replacement. Tell AI whether you want a literal translation, natural language or a formal adaptation. For important specialist material, review the result carefully. Specify the audience, region, formality and whether to preserve idioms or adapt them. Review names, numbers and specialist terms carefully; fluent wording alone cannot confirm that meaning was preserved.
Try it: Tell it whether you want a literal translation or natural everyday language.
LESSON 8

Use AI for creative work

Stories, invitations, captions, jokes, ideas and visual concepts can all be developed collaboratively. Give AI constraints and examples of the style you want.

In depth: Creative collaboration works well when you provide constraints and then explore several concepts. Combine promising elements and refine the chosen direction. AI can generate variations quickly, but human judgement decides which idea is relevant and original. Use the model to explore possibilities, then make deliberate choices about what to combine or reject. Your brief and revisions shape the work; review generated material for originality and fit before sharing or publishing.
Try it: Keep the final creative choices yours. AI can provide options, but your judgement gives the work identity.
LESSON 9

Review before you publish

Ask AI to check a draft for ambiguity, unintended tone, repetition or missing information. Then perform your own final review.

In depth: Before publishing, check names, dates, numbers, links, claims and tone. AI can perform a useful second-pass critique by looking for ambiguity, repetition or unsupported statements, but you should make the final judgement. A final review should include details AI may not know, such as approved wording, permissions and intended audience. Check links and quoted material directly, then read the piece once as a reader would.
Try it: For public communication, also check names, dates, links, numbers and claims manually.
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Create something useful: a message, short article, invitation, plan or presentation outline. Start with your own rough idea, use AI to improve it, request a critique and produce a final version.

In depth: Create one useful piece of communication from your own rough idea. Use AI to structure, improve and critique it, then compare the final version with your original. Revise anything that no longer sounds like you or that needs checking. Keep the final version only after checking that it is accurate, appropriate and still serves your original purpose. Note which AI suggestions improved it and which you chose to reject.
Try it: Compare the final result with your original. If it no longer sounds like you, revise it. The goal is better communication, not replacing your voice.
Do it now: Apply this module to one real, low-risk situation in your life. Finish the task rather than just reading about it.
