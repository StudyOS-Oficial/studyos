MODULE 01 · 10 LESSONS

Getting started with AI

Understand what artificial intelligence is, what ChatGPT actually does, what it can and cannot do, and how to start using it with confidence.

LESSON 1

What is AI, really?

Artificial intelligence is a broad family of technologies that can analyse information, recognise patterns, generate content and assist with decisions or tasks. You do not need to understand the mathematics behind a model to use it well. Think of AI as a tool that can work with language, images, documents and other information. ChatGPT is a conversational AI: you describe what you need in ordinary language and it generates a response based on patterns learned during training and the context you provide.

In depth: AI is a family of computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human cognitive abilities, such as recognising patterns, processing language, generating content and making predictions. Modern generative AI systems such as ChatGPT use neural networks trained on very large amounts of data. They do not think like a human brain and they do not possess human experience or consciousness. A useful mental model is to see AI as a powerful tool that transforms your instructions and available context into an output.
Practical idea: A useful mental model is: AI is not a person sitting behind the screen. It does not have human understanding, intentions or personal experience. It produces an answer from the information and instructions available to it.
LESSON 2

What ChatGPT can do

ChatGPT can explain concepts, rewrite text, brainstorm ideas, summarise information you provide, translate, create plans, compare options and help you practise a skill. It can also work through a problem step by step when you give it enough context.

In depth: ChatGPT is particularly useful when language, structure or ideas are involved. It can explain a difficult concept at different levels, rewrite a text, summarise material, translate, brainstorm, organise information, create plans and help you practise. Depending on the version and tools available, it can also work with images, files, web information and data. The important distinction is between capability and guarantee: being able to generate an answer does not mean every answer is correct or appropriate.
Practical idea: The important point is that the quality of the result depends strongly on the task and the instructions. Asking 'Help me' is possible, but asking 'Explain this letter in simple English and give me three actions I should take' gives the system a much clearer target.
LESSON 3

What AI cannot guarantee

AI can make mistakes. It may misunderstand your request, invent a detail, use outdated information or sound extremely confident while being wrong. This is often called a hallucination. Fluent language is not proof of accuracy.

In depth: A language model can produce an answer that sounds polished even when an important detail is wrong. It may misunderstand ambiguous wording, fill gaps with a plausible guess, reproduce an error from its context or lack access to current information. This is why confidence in the wording should never be confused with evidence. For low-risk creative tasks, an imperfect answer may simply be edited; for health, legal, financial, safety or other consequential matters, important claims should be checked against appropriate sources.
Practical idea: Never treat a generated answer as automatically true. For important information—health, legal, financial, safety, dates, prices or official requirements—use AI as an assistant and verify the critical facts with a reliable source.
LESSON 4

Your first useful conversation

A good first conversation does not require technical vocabulary. Start with your goal, explain the situation briefly and tell ChatGPT what kind of answer you want.

In depth: A conversation with AI works best when you treat the first message as the beginning of a collaboration rather than a test you must pass. State what you are trying to achieve, give the minimum background needed to understand the situation and describe what a useful answer would look like. You can then inspect the response and continue. There is no requirement to know special technical vocabulary: ordinary language is enough. If the first answer is not useful, explain what is missing instead of starting from zero.
Practical idea: Try: 'I have never used AI before. Explain in simple language three things ChatGPT can help me with at home, and give me one example for each.' Then ask a follow-up such as: 'Which one would save me the most time?'
LESSON 5

Give AI a job

One of the easiest ways to improve results is to give the AI a clear role or task. You are not pretending it is a human; you are defining the kind of assistance you want.

In depth: Giving AI a job means defining the function you want it to perform. You might ask it to act as a tutor, editor, planner, interviewer, translator or brainstorming partner. This does not turn the model into a real professional; it simply gives the response a useful frame. A good role instruction is combined with the task, audience and success criteria. The more clearly you define the job, the less the model has to guess about what you expect.
Practical idea: For example: 'Act as a patient tutor. Explain photosynthesis to a 15-year-old using one everyday analogy, then ask me two questions to check whether I understood.' The role, audience and desired output make the task clearer.
LESSON 6

Context changes the answer

Context is the information that helps AI understand your situation. Include only what is useful: your goal, audience, constraints, preferences, starting level and relevant facts.

In depth: Context is the information that changes how a request should be interpreted. The same question can produce very different useful answers depending on your age, experience, budget, deadline, audience, preferences or constraints. Good context is relevant rather than exhaustive: include what affects the task and leave out sensitive information that is unnecessary. Think of it as giving the assistant the pieces of the situation that a person would normally need before helping you.
Practical idea: Compare: 'Plan a trip to Italy' with 'Plan a four-day trip to northern Italy for two adults in May. We prefer trains, moderate walking and a mid-range budget.' The second request gives the AI boundaries that make a useful answer possible.
LESSON 7

Ask for a format

You can control not only what AI says, but how it presents the information. Ask for a checklist, table, numbered steps, short summary, email draft, shopping list or another structure.

In depth: The format of an answer is part of the task. A beginner may understand a numbered explanation better than a dense paragraph; a shopping decision may be easier as a comparison table; a recurring task may work better as a checklist. Asking for a format reduces the work required after the answer is generated. You can also combine format and length: for example, request five steps, one example per step and a short warning at the end.
Practical idea: If you are overwhelmed by a long answer, say: 'Reduce this to five bullet points and put the actions in priority order.' Good prompting is often just good communication.
LESSON 8

Improve instead of restarting

Your first answer does not have to be perfect. Treat the conversation as an editing process. Tell the AI exactly what should change.

In depth: AI conversations are iterative. Your first response is often a draft that reveals what the model understood and what still needs changing. Instead of repeating the whole request, point to the specific problem: too long, too technical, too formal, missing an example, or aimed at the wrong audience. This feedback acts like an editing instruction. Iterative refinement is a normal part of effective prompting, not evidence that you failed to ask correctly the first time.
Practical idea: Useful follow-ups include: 'Make it shorter', 'Use simpler words', 'Give me two alternatives', 'Remove the technical language', 'Add an example', and 'Ask me three questions before you answer.'
LESSON 9

Use AI without giving away control

AI should support your judgement, not replace it. You decide what information to share, which suggestions make sense and what actions to take. If a recommendation affects money, health, safety or another important person, slow down and check it.

In depth: Using AI well means keeping the human decision-maker in the loop. The model can generate possibilities, organise information and expose options, but you decide whether the information is relevant, whether the recommendation fits your circumstances and whether an action should actually be taken. This becomes especially important when the consequences are significant. A useful habit is to separate three questions: what did the AI produce, what needs verification, and what decision is mine to make?
Practical idea: A strong habit is the three-step check: What did AI say? What evidence supports it? What decision am I actually making?
LESSON 10

Final practical challenge

Choose one ordinary task you genuinely have today: writing a message, planning dinner, organising a weekend, understanding a document or learning a topic. Give ChatGPT the goal, useful context and desired format. Then improve the answer once.

In depth: The purpose of this module is not to memorise definitions but to experience the basic AI workflow: define a goal, provide context, request an appropriate output, inspect it and refine it. Choose a low-risk task that is genuinely useful to you. Notice which information you had to provide for the answer to become specific. Then make one deliberate improvement to your prompt. This small experiment teaches more than simply reading a list of prompting rules.
Practical idea: Write down what changed between your first and second prompt. The goal is not to produce a perfect prompt. The goal is to experience how clearer instructions produce more useful assistance.
Do it now: Apply the technique to one real situation from your own life. Keep the task simple enough to finish in 10–15 minutes.

Remember: AI is most useful when you stay in control, provide good instructions and review what comes back.
