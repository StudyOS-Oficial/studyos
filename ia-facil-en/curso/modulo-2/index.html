MODULE 02 · 10 LESSONS

Your first conversation with ChatGPT

Learn how a good conversation works: make a request, provide context, inspect the answer, ask follow-up questions and gradually reach the result you actually need.

LESSON 1

Start with a clear goal

Before typing, ask yourself: 'What do I want at the end?' A goal such as 'write a friendly message to my neighbour about the noise' is more useful than simply saying 'write something'.

Practical idea: If the goal is unclear, ask AI to help define it: 'I know I need to organise my finances, but I do not know where to start. Ask me five questions and then suggest a simple first step.'
LESSON 2

Tell it who the answer is for

The same information can need a completely different explanation depending on the audience. Tell ChatGPT whether you are writing for yourself, a child, a customer, a teacher, a colleague or a friend.

Practical idea: Try: 'Explain this technical paragraph to someone with no background in the subject.' Then ask for a second version aimed at an expert. Notice how the vocabulary and depth change.
LESSON 3

Give constraints

Constraints make an answer practical. You can specify length, budget, time, tone, ingredients, available equipment, language or anything else that matters.

Practical idea: Example: 'Give me three dinner ideas for two people. Each should take less than 25 minutes, use ordinary supermarket ingredients and cost as little as possible.'
LESSON 4

Ask one task at a time when needed

AI can handle complex requests, but beginners often get better results by separating a large job into stages. First ask for a plan, then ask it to execute each part.

Practical idea: For a presentation, try: 'First give me a five-slide structure.' After reviewing it, say: 'Now write slide 2.' This gives you more control than requesting an entire presentation at once.
LESSON 5

Use follow-up questions

A conversation is valuable because you do not have to repeat everything. Once the relevant context is established, you can refine the result through follow-ups.

Practical idea: Ask: 'What would you change?', 'Can you make it warmer?', 'What am I missing?', or 'Give me an alternative for someone with less time.' Follow-ups turn a generic answer into a personalised one.
LESSON 6

Correct misunderstandings

If ChatGPT misunderstands you, do not assume the whole conversation is ruined. State the correction explicitly and continue.

Practical idea: For example: 'I meant a weekend trip by train, not a day trip by car. Keep the same budget and change only the transport and itinerary.' Clear corrections save time.
LESSON 7

Ask AI to ask you questions

Sometimes you do not know which details matter. Reverse the process: ask ChatGPT what information it needs before producing the answer.

Practical idea: Prompt: 'Before recommending a laptop, ask me the seven questions you need to know about how I will use it.' Answer the questions, then request the recommendation.
LESSON 8

Separate ideas from facts

AI is particularly useful for generating options and drafts. It is more risky when a response contains factual claims you need to rely on. Tell it which kind of task you want.

Practical idea: For brainstorming: 'Give me 15 possible names.' For factual work: 'List the claims that need verification and tell me what source type I should check.'
LESSON 9

Know when to start a new chat

A long conversation can accumulate context that is no longer relevant. Starting a new chat can make a task cleaner, especially when switching from one subject to another.

Practical idea: Keep a conversation when the history helps. Start fresh when the old context could confuse the new task. You can always paste the small amount of information that matters.
LESSON 10

Final practical challenge

Have a five-minute conversation with ChatGPT about a real task. First state the goal. Then add two constraints. Ask for a first answer, critique it, and request a revised version.

Practical idea: At the end, compare the first and final answers. Identify the single instruction that made the biggest difference. That is the beginning of your personal prompting style.
Do it now: Apply the technique to one real situation from your own life. Keep the task simple enough to finish in 10–15 minutes.
FINAL CHECK

Put the module into practice

Before moving on, answer these questions for yourself:

  1. What is the most useful idea from this module?
  2. Where could you apply it this week?
  3. What would you need to verify before trusting the result?

Remember: AI is most useful when you stay in control, provide good instructions and review what comes back.
