MODULE 13 · 10 LESSONS · BONUS

Think smarter with AI · Bonus

Use AI to improve the way you reason: clarify assumptions, explore alternatives, test arguments and reflect on decisions without handing over your judgement.

LESSON 1

AI as a thinking partner

AI can help you articulate ideas, but its fluent wording can make weak reasoning sound convincing. Separate clarity from truth.

In depth: AI can help make an idea clearer, expose alternatives and organise an argument, but fluent wording can make weak reasoning sound convincing. Separate clarity from truth. Ask AI to restate your argument and identify what evidence would be needed before accepting its conclusions. Use AI to make reasoning visible: ask it to restate the argument, identify evidence and flag gaps. Then inspect the reasoning yourself; clarity is useful, but it does not establish that a conclusion is true.
Try it: Ask AI to explain your argument back to you before suggesting improvements.
LESSON 2

Find hidden assumptions

Many mistakes begin with assumptions that were never stated. Ask AI to identify them so you can decide whether they are justified.

In depth: Many conclusions depend on assumptions that were never stated. Ask AI to list them explicitly and identify which ones could be wrong. Making assumptions visible gives you something concrete to verify rather than arguing about a conclusion in the abstract. Ask which assumptions are essential and which are merely convenient. Check the important ones against evidence or your own circumstances before relying on the conclusion.
Try it: Try: 'List the assumptions behind my plan. Mark which ones I should verify.'
LESSON 3

Argue both sides

Exploring opposing arguments can reveal weaknesses and make your reasoning stronger.

In depth: Considering the strongest opposing argument is a useful stress test. Ask AI to defend the other position fairly rather than creating a weak version that is easy to dismiss. Then compare the evidence and identify which points genuinely remain uncertain. Ask for the strongest fair case on each side, including the evidence each would need. Avoid treating a balanced-sounding answer as proof that the evidence is equally strong.
Try it: Ask for the strongest argument against your position before finalising it.
LESSON 4

Separate facts from opinions

A useful discussion distinguishes evidence, interpretation and personal preference.

In depth: A useful analysis distinguishes evidence, interpretation and preference. Ask AI to label claims according to those categories, then verify factual claims that matter. This prevents a confident opinion from being mistaken for established evidence. Some statements mix evidence and evaluation. Break them into smaller claims, verify factual parts and identify the values or preferences behind the judgement.
Try it: Ask AI to label each claim as fact, inference or opinion, then verify factual claims.
LESSON 5

Look for missing information

A decision can feel clear simply because important alternatives were ignored. Ask what information would change the conclusion.

In depth: A decision may feel obvious simply because important information was never considered. Ask what facts, alternatives or constraints could materially change the conclusion. This turns uncertainty into a list of questions you can investigate. Prioritise missing facts that could change the decision rather than collecting information endlessly. Decide what evidence would resolve the uncertainty and where you can obtain it.
Try it: Try: 'What do I not know that could materially change this decision?'
LESSON 6

Use counterexamples

A single counterexample can expose an overly broad rule. Ask AI to test your statement against unusual or opposing cases.

In depth: A broad rule can look convincing until you find a case where it fails. Ask AI to produce counterexamples and unusual cases. A counterexample does not automatically prove the opposite; it shows that your original statement may need a narrower definition or qualification. Test whether the counterexample really fits the rule and its scope. If it does, refine the claim by adding a condition or narrowing its meaning instead of ignoring the exception.
Try it: Give it your rule and ask for three cases where it might fail.
LESSON 7

Avoid confirmation bias

If you only ask AI to support your preferred answer, you may reinforce your own bias.

In depth: If you ask only for support, AI can become an echo of your existing preference. Deliberately request objections, contrary evidence and situations where your conclusion would be wrong. This does not guarantee objectivity, but it creates a healthier challenge to your initial position. Ask the same standard of evidence for the position you favour and the position you oppose. Notice when you accept weak support for one side but demand unusually strong proof from the other.
Try it: Prompt: 'Do not agree with me automatically. Look for evidence that could prove my conclusion wrong.'
LESSON 8

Think before accepting a summary

A summary can remove nuance. For important material, compare the summary with the original and ask what may have been lost.

In depth: Summarisation removes information by design. Nuance, exceptions and uncertainty can disappear even when the summary sounds excellent. For important material, compare the summary with the source and ask what was omitted or simplified. Compare the summary with the source for qualifiers, uncertainty, minority views and causal claims. Ask for a list of omissions, but verify those omissions against the original material.
Try it: Ask: 'What important qualifications or exceptions might this summary hide?'
LESSON 9

Make a final judgement

AI can structure evidence and arguments, but responsibility for important choices remains human.

In depth: AI can organise evidence and arguments, but responsibility for important choices remains with the person making the decision. Before acting, formulate your own conclusion and explain why. If the AI disagrees, treat that disagreement as something to investigate rather than as an automatic correction. State the evidence and uncertainty behind your decision in your own words. You remain responsible for the choice, including deciding when the evidence is not strong enough to act.
Try it: Before acting, write your own conclusion in one or two sentences and explain why.
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Take a real low-risk opinion or decision. Ask AI to identify assumptions, argue the opposite side, find missing information and test your reasoning. Then make your own conclusion.

In depth: Choose a low-risk opinion or decision. Ask AI to identify assumptions, argue the opposite side, find missing information and produce counterexamples. Then write your own conclusion independently. The exercise is successful if your reasoning becomes clearer, not if AI chooses for you. After reaching your own conclusion, note what evidence would make you revise it. Being willing to update is a stronger outcome than trying to defend an initial opinion at all costs.
Try it: Keep the final decision independent of the AI's confidence or wording.
