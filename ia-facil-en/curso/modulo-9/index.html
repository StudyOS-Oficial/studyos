MODULE 09 · 10 LESSONS

Organize your life with AI

Turn tasks, priorities, routines and plans into practical systems while keeping control of your time and decisions.

LESSON 1

Start with everything on your mind

When a list feels overwhelming, paste the tasks into AI and ask it to organise them without inventing new obligations.

In depth: A planning conversation can begin with an unstructured list. Give AI the tasks, commitments and concerns exactly as they occur to you, then ask it to organise them. This separates capture from prioritisation and reduces the mental effort of turning a messy list into a workable plan.
Try it: Prompt: 'Group this list into urgent, important and optional. Do not delete anything and do not add tasks.'
LESSON 2

Prioritise realistically

Not everything can be urgent. Give AI deadlines, consequences, effort and dependencies, then ask it to propose an order.

In depth: Prioritisation means deciding what deserves attention first. Ask AI to distinguish urgency, importance, effort and consequences. A realistic plan also recognises limited time and energy; if everything is labelled high priority, the prioritisation has not actually happened.
Try it: Review the proposed order yourself. AI does not know the emotional or practical consequences unless you explain them.
LESSON 3

Build a simple routine

AI can convert recurring responsibilities into a weekly routine. Tell it what is fixed and what is flexible.

In depth: A routine works when it is easy enough to repeat. AI can help identify recurring actions, suggest a sequence and create a checklist. Avoid building a complicated system that takes more effort to maintain than the task itself. Start small and adjust after using it.
Try it: Ask for a plan with buffer time. A schedule that leaves no room for reality is not a useful schedule.
LESSON 4

Plan a project

For a larger goal, ask AI to break it into milestones, tasks and dependencies. Then identify the first action you can actually do.

In depth: Projects become manageable when a large goal is divided into milestones and actions. Ask AI to define the desired outcome, dependencies, deadlines and possible risks. Then review the plan against reality before committing to it, because the model does not know every constraint in your life.
Try it: Prompt: 'Turn this goal into four milestones. For each, give me the next physical action and a reasonable time estimate.'
LESSON 5

Prepare for a busy week

Give AI your fixed appointments, deadlines and priorities. Ask for a draft schedule that protects rest and travel time.

In depth: A weekly plan should combine fixed commitments with flexible tasks. Ask AI to group similar activities, identify preparation that can happen early and highlight overloaded days. The result should be a realistic map rather than an ideal schedule that collapses after one interruption.
Try it: Do not include sensitive calendar information that is unnecessary. General descriptions are usually enough.
LESSON 6

Create checklists

Checklists are excellent for repeatable tasks: travel preparation, weekly shopping, home maintenance or preparing a meeting.

In depth: Checklists externalise memory. They are especially useful for recurring processes such as travel preparation, events, household tasks or administrative work. Ask AI to group steps logically and include final checks. Then test the checklist once and remove anything that is unnecessary.
Try it: Ask for a checklist that is short enough to use. Then edit it until it matches your real routine.
LESSON 7

Reduce decision fatigue

For repeated low-risk decisions, create rules. AI can help you design them, such as a meal rotation or packing checklist.

In depth: Many small choices consume attention. AI can reduce this burden by creating defaults, shortlists and simple rules. The important step is to decide your criteria first so the model does not silently choose them for you. A shortlist should simplify your decision, not hide it.
Try it: A good system reduces the number of decisions you need to make, but you should still review the rules when circumstances change.
LESSON 8

Prepare conversations and meetings

AI can help you build an agenda, questions, talking points or a concise summary. Keep confidential information out unless it is genuinely necessary and appropriate.

In depth: AI can help prepare an agenda, questions, talking points or possible responses. Give it the purpose of the conversation and the relationship involved. Use the output as preparation, not a script that must be followed regardless of what the other person actually says.
Try it: Prompt: 'Turn these notes into a 20-minute meeting agenda with three decisions we need to make.'
LESSON 9

Review and adapt

A plan is a tool, not a promise. Ask AI to review what worked, what failed and what should change next week.

In depth: Planning is not a one-time prediction of the future. After using a plan, compare what happened with what was expected. Ask AI to identify recurring problems and propose a simpler adjustment. Review turns experience into better planning rather than repeating the same assumptions.
Try it: A useful weekly review has three questions: What worked? What got in the way? What is one change worth testing?
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Give AI a messy list of ten real but non-sensitive tasks. Ask it to prioritise them, build a realistic sequence and identify the first three actions.

In depth: Take one upcoming week or project and build a practical plan with AI. Start with an unstructured list, prioritise it, create a sequence and add a short checklist. Afterward, review what the plan assumed and what you would change next time.
Try it: Then remove one task and see how the plan changes. You are learning to use AI as a planning assistant—not as the person in charge of your life.
Do it now: Apply this module to one real, low-risk situation in your life. Finish the task rather than just reading about it.
