MODULE 06 · 10 LESSONS

Do not believe everything at first

Learn to recognise uncertainty, detect errors and verify important information before you rely on an AI answer.

LESSON 1

AI can sound confident and still be wrong

A fluent answer is not evidence that it is true. AI generates likely language; it does not automatically check every statement against reality.

Try it: Ask: 'Which parts of this answer are facts, and which parts could be uncertain?' Use that as a starting point for verification.
LESSON 2

Spot warning signs

Be cautious when an answer gives very precise facts without sources, makes strong claims about a current situation, or avoids admitting uncertainty.

Try it: A useful prompt is: 'List any claims in your previous answer that I should verify independently.'
LESSON 3

Ask for sources carefully

AI may provide source names or links, but you should still open and check important sources yourself. A citation is useful only if it actually supports the claim.

Try it: For important research, ask: 'For each factual claim, tell me what type of primary or official source I should verify it with.'
LESSON 4

Separate facts from assumptions

A good answer can contain a mixture of known information, reasonable assumptions and suggestions. Ask AI to label those categories.

Try it: Try: 'Separate your response into confirmed facts, assumptions and recommendations. Do not present assumptions as facts.'
LESSON 5

Check current information

Schedules, prices, laws, product availability, public figures and many other facts can change. AI knowledge may not reflect the latest update.

Try it: When something is time-sensitive, verify it with the organisation or source responsible for the information before acting.
LESSON 6

Use two-step verification

For a consequential question, first ask AI to explain the issue. Then independently check the key claim using a reliable source.

Try it: The goal is not to distrust everything. It is to match the amount of checking to the consequences of being wrong.
LESSON 7

Be especially careful with health, money and legal matters

AI can help you understand terminology or prepare questions, but it should not replace qualified professional advice where the stakes are high.

Try it: You can ask: 'Explain this medical term in plain language and give me questions to ask my doctor.' That is safer than asking AI to diagnose you.
LESSON 8

Challenge the answer

Ask AI to argue against its own conclusion. This can reveal assumptions and missing alternatives, although it is not a guarantee of correctness.

Try it: Prompt: 'What is the strongest reason this recommendation could be wrong? What information would change it?'
LESSON 9

Build a verification habit

Create a simple rule: low-risk everyday tasks may need little checking; high-impact decisions deserve stronger verification.

Try it: A practical scale is: convenience → quick review; important decision → source check; high-stakes decision → qualified professional or official source.
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Take one AI answer you received recently. Highlight three factual claims and decide how you would verify each one.

Try it: Then ask ChatGPT to identify possible weaknesses. Compare its warnings with your own checks. The skill is knowing when an answer is good enough and when it is not.
Do it now: Apply this module to one real, low-risk situation in your life. Finish the task rather than just reading about it.
