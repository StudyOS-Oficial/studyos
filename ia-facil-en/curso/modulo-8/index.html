MODULE 08 · 10 LESSONS

Learn with AI

Turn ChatGPT into a study companion for understanding, practising, reviewing and learning at your own pace.

LESSON 1

AI as a tutor, not an answer machine

The best learning use is often interaction: explanation, practice, feedback and correction. Simply asking for the answer can remove the part of learning that matters.

In depth: A tutor helps you understand why an answer works, not only what the answer is. Ask AI to explain, question you and adapt its difficulty instead of immediately solving every exercise. This keeps you involved in the learning process and makes the interaction more useful over time.
Try it: Tell AI: 'Do not give me the answer immediately. Ask me questions and guide me to it.'
LESSON 2

Start at your level

A beginner and an expert need different explanations. Tell AI what you already know and where you are struggling.

In depth: Learning is most effective when the explanation matches what you already know. Tell AI your level, what you understand and where you are confused. A beginner may need definitions and analogies, while an advanced learner may need exceptions, mechanisms and challenging problems.
Try it: Prompt: 'I understand the basic idea but I get confused by these two concepts. Explain the difference using one analogy and one example.'
LESSON 3

Ask for a lesson

You can request a short lesson with a clear sequence: explanation, example, practice and recap.

In depth: You can turn a vague topic into a structured mini-lesson. Ask for a short explanation, key concepts, an example, a question to check understanding and a small exercise. This creates a learning sequence instead of a wall of information.
Try it: Try: 'Teach me this topic in 15 minutes. Explain one idea at a time, then give me a question before moving on.'
LESSON 4

Use active recall

Learning improves when you try to retrieve information rather than only reread it. Ask AI to quiz you without showing the answers first.

In depth: Active recall means trying to retrieve information from memory rather than simply rereading it. Ask AI to quiz you without immediately showing the answer. After you respond, request feedback and an explanation of mistakes. This makes the learner perform part of the cognitive work.
Try it: Prompt: 'Give me five questions from easy to difficult. Wait for my answer to each one and explain my mistakes.'
LESSON 5

Ask for feedback

Good feedback explains what was correct, what needs improvement and how to improve it. Tell AI what kind of feedback you want.

In depth: Useful feedback identifies what was correct, what was missing and how to improve. Ask AI to explain the reason for an error rather than simply giving the correct answer. For important subjects, compare the feedback with trusted course material because AI can also make mistakes.
Try it: For writing: 'Correct the three most important mistakes first. Explain why each matters, then let me try again.'
LESSON 6

Change the explanation

If an explanation does not work, change the representation: analogy, diagram description, example, simpler language or step-by-step reasoning.

In depth: When one explanation does not work, change the representation. Ask for an analogy, a simpler version, a concrete example, a diagram description or a step-by-step explanation. Difficulty understanding a concept does not always mean you need more information; sometimes you need a different explanation.
Try it: You can say: 'I still do not understand. Explain it as if I were 12, then give me a real-world example.'
LESSON 7

Practise progressively

Ask for exercises that become harder as you improve. This creates a learning path instead of random questions.

In depth: Progressive practice moves from simple examples to more demanding problems. Start with guided exercises, then remove some of the support and finally attempt a problem independently. AI can generate this progression and adjust it according to your performance.
Try it: Prompt: 'Give me one problem. If I solve it correctly, increase the difficulty slightly. If I make a mistake, give me a hint rather than the answer.'
LESSON 8

Use AI to organise study

AI can turn a syllabus or notes into a revision plan, checklist or set of questions. Check that the plan covers what your teacher or course actually requires.

In depth: AI can help turn notes into outlines, flashcards, question banks, revision plans or topic checklists. Organisation is useful, but it should not replace actual study. Always compare generated summaries with your original material when accuracy matters.
Try it: Give it your topics and deadline, then ask for a realistic schedule with review sessions.
LESSON 9

Do not outsource your thinking

For school or professional work, use AI to learn and improve rather than submit work you do not understand. Your own reasoning matters.

In depth: If AI performs every step, you may finish the task without learning the underlying skill. Use it strategically: attempt the problem first, ask for hints when stuck, then compare your reasoning with the explanation. The objective is greater independence, not permanent dependence on the tool.
Try it: Ask AI to critique your answer after you have attempted it. That preserves the useful struggle that creates learning.
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Choose a topic you genuinely want to learn. Ask AI to teach it interactively, answer its questions yourself and request feedback.

In depth: Choose one topic you are learning. Ask AI to teach it at your level, quiz you, review your answers and then give you a harder exercise. Notice which parts you solved yourself and which parts you delegated. That distinction is important for real learning.
Try it: At the end, explain the topic back in your own words. If you cannot, ask for one more explanation and try again.
Do it now: Apply this module to one real, low-risk situation in your life. Finish the task rather than just reading about it.
