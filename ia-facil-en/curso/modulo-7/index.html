MODULE 07 · 10 LESSONS

Protect your data

Learn what to share with AI, what to keep private, and how to recognise suspicious requests, scams and unsafe situations.

LESSON 1

Think before you paste

A chatbot is not the right place for every piece of information. Avoid sharing secrets, passwords, full payment details, private keys or unnecessary identifying information.

In depth: Before sharing information with an AI system, ask whether it actually needs it. Remove names, addresses and identifying details when they are unnecessary. Data minimisation is a simple privacy principle: provide the smallest amount of information that still allows the task to be completed.
Try it: Before sending text, ask: 'Does the AI need this exact detail to complete my task?' If not, remove or generalise it.
LESSON 2

Use placeholders

You can often replace personal information with neutral labels while keeping the task useful.

In depth: Placeholders preserve the useful structure of a prompt without exposing unnecessary personal information. Replace a real name with [NAME], an address with [ADDRESS] or another identifying detail with a neutral label. This is especially useful when asking AI to rewrite or analyse a document.
Try it: Instead of pasting a real account number, write '[ACCOUNT NUMBER]' and ask AI to help with the structure of the message.
LESSON 3

Protect passwords and codes

Never paste passwords, authentication codes, recovery codes or private keys into a chatbot. These are credentials, not ordinary context.

In depth: Credentials and authentication secrets should remain secret. If you need help with an error, share the error message while removing passwords, access codes and private tokens. A helpful AI response is never worth exposing a credential that could give someone access to an account.
Try it: If a message asks you to reveal a code so someone can 'verify' your account, stop and use the official service independently.
LESSON 4

Be careful with documents

Documents may contain hidden or unexpected personal information. Review what you upload and share only what is necessary.

In depth: Documents can contain names, addresses, signatures, account details or other information you did not notice. Before uploading one, consider whether every part is necessary. If only a paragraph is relevant, sharing that smaller section can reduce unnecessary exposure.
Try it: For a letter, you can often remove names, addresses, reference numbers and signatures before asking AI to explain the text.
LESSON 5

Recognise urgency and pressure

Scams often try to make you act quickly: 'Pay now', 'Your account will close', 'Do not tell anyone'. AI can help analyse a suspicious message, but do not click its links while investigating.

In depth: Scams often use urgency: “act now”, “keep this secret” or “send the code immediately.” Pressure is a reason to slow down. AI can help you analyse a suspicious message, but important requests should be verified independently rather than trusted because the message sounds convincing.
Try it: Copy the message text without the link and ask: 'What signs of phishing or social engineering do you see?' Then contact the organisation through an official channel.
LESSON 6

Check the sender independently

A logo, name or familiar-looking address does not prove a message is genuine. Open the official website yourself rather than using the contact details in a suspicious message.

In depth: A message can appear to come from a bank, company or family member while actually being fraudulent. Do not rely only on links or phone numbers contained in a suspicious message. Use an independently known website, application or contact method to verify the request.
Try it: This simple habit prevents many mistakes: verify the situation through a channel you found independently.
LESSON 7

Understand sensitive information

Health, financial, legal, identity and intimate information deserves extra care. Minimise what you share and consider whether a local or offline solution is better.

In depth: Sensitive information is broader than passwords. Health details, financial information, identity documents, private conversations and precise location can create privacy risks. The goal is not necessarily to avoid AI completely; it is to understand what information is necessary and minimise what you share.
Try it: If AI only needs to understand a situation, describe the relevant facts without unnecessary identifying details.
LESSON 8

AI can help with safety—but verify

You can use AI to create a checklist, explain scam patterns or draft a cautious reply. Do not let it make a high-risk decision based on incomplete information.

In depth: AI can explain warning signs, organise a safety checklist or help formulate questions. It can also misunderstand a situation or lack critical context. In emergencies, security incidents or other high-risk situations, follow relevant official or professional guidance instead of treating AI as the final authority.
Try it: Prompt: 'Give me a safety checklist for evaluating this message. Do not tell me it is definitely legitimate unless there is evidence.'
LESSON 9

Create your personal privacy rule

A simple rule is: share the minimum information necessary, remove secrets, anonymise personal details and verify important requests independently.

In depth: A short personal rule is easier to remember than a long policy. For example: never share credentials, remove unnecessary identifying details, independently verify sensitive requests and slow down when someone creates artificial urgency. Consistent habits are a strong privacy defence.
Try it: Write your own four-word reminder: 'Minimum data. No secrets. Verify.' Put it somewhere you will see it.
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Take a fictional suspicious message and practise analysing it without clicking anything. Identify the requested action, the pressure tactic, the information being requested and the safe verification route.

In depth: Take a typical AI task and rewrite the prompt to minimise personal information. Replace identifying details with placeholders and identify what information is genuinely necessary. Then decide what independent verification you would use if the situation involved money, accounts or safety.
Try it: The goal is not to become paranoid. It is to become slower and more deliberate when someone—or something—asks for sensitive information.
Do it now: Apply this module to one real, low-risk situation in your life. Finish the task rather than just reading about it.
