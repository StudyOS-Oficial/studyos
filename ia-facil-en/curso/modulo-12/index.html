MODULE 12 · 10 LESSONS · BONUS

Practical AI workflows · Bonus

Turn individual AI tricks into repeatable workflows for planning, research, decisions and everyday projects. The goal is a reliable process you can reuse.

LESSON 1

From task to workflow

A workflow is a sequence of repeatable steps rather than a single prompt. Define the input, process, checks and final output.

In depth: A workflow is a repeatable sequence rather than one isolated prompt. Define the input, the steps AI should help with, the checks that matter and the final output. This makes a task easier to repeat and improve. Write down the inputs and final output in plain language before designing the steps. A workflow is easier to evaluate when you can tell whether it produced the intended result.
Try it: Write four steps for a task you repeat every week, then decide where AI can help.
LESSON 2

Plan before you generate

Start by deciding what success looks like. Ask AI to propose a plan before producing a large document or list.

In depth: Before asking for a large output, define what success looks like. Ask for a plan or outline first, review it and then generate the detailed material. This prevents spending time expanding a structure that was wrong from the beginning. Agree on the outline before asking for a long response. If the plan misses a requirement, correct it early; changing direction after a large output is created costs more time.
Try it: Try: 'Before writing anything, give me a five-step plan and wait for my approval.'
LESSON 3

Research with questions

Use AI to identify what you need to know, create search questions and organise information. For important facts, verify against reliable sources.

In depth: AI can help identify what you need to know, turn a broad topic into research questions and organise information. For important factual work, use appropriate sources to verify the claims rather than treating the model as the evidence itself. Turn each research question into a claim that can be checked against a source. Keep notes on where a fact came from and when it was published, especially when the information can change.
Try it: Ask AI to produce a research checklist rather than pretending it is the source of truth.
LESSON 4

Compare options

AI can structure a comparison using criteria you choose. It should not silently decide which criteria matter most.

In depth: A useful comparison starts with criteria. Give AI the factors that matter, such as cost, time, reliability and convenience, and ask it to show trade-offs. This makes the reasoning visible instead of hiding the decision inside a single recommendation. Ask AI to show missing information and assumptions for each option, not only pros and cons. If the evidence is incomplete, treat the comparison as a map of what to investigate next.
Try it: Give it your criteria: cost, time, reliability and convenience, then ask for a comparison table.
LESSON 5

Make checklists

Checklists turn complicated tasks into visible actions and reduce the chance of forgetting steps.

In depth: Checklists turn complicated tasks into visible actions. Ask AI to group steps logically and include final checks. Then test the checklist in a real situation and remove steps that are unnecessary or add missing steps you discovered. A good checklist has a clear point of use and a defined completion condition. Try it during the task, then revise steps that were ambiguous, redundant or easy to overlook.
Try it: Ask: 'Create a checklist for preparing this trip, grouped into documents, packing, transport and final checks.'
LESSON 6

Create decision frameworks

For a difficult choice, ask AI to identify options, criteria, trade-offs and unanswered questions. Keep the final decision yours.

In depth: A decision framework separates options, criteria, trade-offs, risks and unanswered questions. AI can help build this structure without being responsible for the final choice. The framework is valuable because it makes the reasoning easier to inspect. Use criteria that reflect your own goals and constraints. You can ask AI to organise trade-offs or identify questions, but make the final choice after checking the evidence that matters most.
Try it: Try: 'Help me think through this decision without choosing for me.'
LESSON 7

Review the workflow

After completing a task, ask what could be improved next time. A good workflow becomes easier with repetition.

In depth: After completing a workflow, review what caused confusion, delay or repeated corrections. Ask AI to suggest improvements to the process itself. A good workflow should become simpler and more reliable with experience. Look for the step that caused the most delay or errors, then change one part at a time. This makes it easier to see whether the change actually improved the process.
Try it: Ask: 'What part of this process caused the most confusion, and how should I change the template?'
LESSON 8

Use templates

Reusable prompts save time and improve consistency. Keep placeholders obvious so you know what to change.

In depth: Reusable templates reduce repeated work. Keep stable instructions in the template and use clear placeholders for changing information. Review the template occasionally so it does not preserve outdated assumptions. Use descriptive placeholder names and include brief instructions for filling them in. Test the template with a realistic example and remove any field that does not affect the result.
Try it: Create a template with fields such as [GOAL], [AUDIENCE], [DEADLINE] and [CONSTRAINTS].
LESSON 9

Add verification points

A reliable workflow includes moments where you stop and check facts, numbers, names or decisions before moving forward.

In depth: A reliable workflow includes deliberate moments to check facts, numbers, names and important decisions. Verification should happen before an error becomes part of the final output. Mark high-risk steps explicitly so they are not skipped. Put checks before steps where errors become costly, such as before sending, publishing or making a decision. State what counts as verified so the checkpoint is more than a quick glance.
Try it: Mark high-risk steps explicitly: 'Verify this before continuing.'
LESSON 10

Final challenge

Choose one recurring task and turn it into a five-step AI workflow with inputs, prompts, verification points and a final human review.

In depth: Choose one recurring task and turn it into a short workflow. Define inputs, steps, prompts, verification points and a final human review. Run it once, note what failed and improve the workflow before using it again. Run the workflow on a real, low-risk task and record corrections or missing inputs. Make one improvement, then repeat it to see whether the workflow is clearer and more dependable.
Try it: Run the workflow once, note what failed, and improve the template for next time.
